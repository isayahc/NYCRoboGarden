01 / START WITH THE GROWER

A real bottleneck.
A useful place to start.

During the hackathon, Judith “Judi” Desire, founder and executive director of Uptown & Boogie Healthy Project, described how manually sowing individual seedling-tray cells can take hours.

That conversation shaped our first task. We’re exploring a configurable robotic workstation that can locate seeds, place them into tray cells, check the result, and recover from misses.

The goal is to free time for growing and community work—and make the same equipment a hands-on way to learn robotics and AI.

04 / BUILD, LEARN, SHARE

Tools communities
can make their own.

Our longer-term vision is affordable, reusable equipment that growers and learners can configure, maintain, and share.

Seed placement provides a tangible learning path: define a task, change a simulation, inspect camera images, calibrate an arm, and measure the outcome. Community workshops and shared equipment are possibilities we want to explore.

Affordability needs evidence, too. A specialized seeder may be the better tool. We’re asking where adaptable robotics can earn its place.
